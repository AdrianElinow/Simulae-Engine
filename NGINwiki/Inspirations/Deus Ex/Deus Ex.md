# Deus Ex

Deus Ex is the classic conspiracy-driven immersive sim about infiltration, augmentation, and conversation-driven mission solving.
It is one of the clearest benchmarks for how Simulae should handle multiple solutions to a single objective without hard scripting.

## Core Loop
1. Enter a mission through a hub, briefing, or story lead.
2. Gather information through dialogue, terminals, documents, and exploration.
3. Choose stealth, hacking, social access, or direct force to solve the objective.
4. Carry the consequences into later missions, hubs, or faction relationships.

## Required Systems
- Hub-based mission structure with optional side content.
- Augmentations that unlock build-dependent traversal, combat, and utility options.
- Hacking, keypads, locks, and security devices as first-class access gates.
- Branching dialogue and quest acceptance that can alter available routes.
- Lethal and nonlethal approaches that stay valid across the same map.

## Factions
- UNATCO: the opening authority structure and default player employer.
- NSF: the early insurgent opposition and anti-corporate pressure group.
- Illuminati: the hidden old-world power layer behind the conspiracy.
- Majestic 12: the corporate-intelligence network that drives the main plot.
- Triads: local criminal and information brokers in Hong Kong.
- FEMA and allied agencies: later-stage state pressure and enforcement.

## Signature NPCs
- JC Denton: the player character and blank-slate augmented agent.
- Paul Denton: JC's brother and a critical moral counterweight.
- Joseph Manderley: the UNATCO commander who frames the early mission set.
- Anna Navarre and Gunther Hermann: elite UNATCO operatives who show the agency's hard edge.
- Tracer Tong: the hacker and underground ally who helps route the conspiracy.
- Morgan Everett: the Illuminati power broker.
- Walton Simons: the enforcement face of the growing authoritarian machine.
- Bob Page: the corporate apex of Majestic 12.
- Daedalus and Icarus: the competing AI presences behind the information war.
- Nicolette DuClare: an example of social access and secret-society pressure.

## Signature Augmentations
- Cloak: temporary invisibility and stealth routing.
- Speed: movement burst for repositioning or escape.
- Regeneration: survivability and resource-efficient recovery.
- Vision Enhancement: environmental awareness and target spotting.
- Ballistic Protection: defensive play and direct-combat resilience.
- Microfibral Muscle: movement, lifting, and physical access.
- Targeting and combat augments: make weapon builds feel meaningfully different.

## Signature Gear
- GEP Gun: the classic explosive problem solver.
- Mini crossbow: quiet takedowns and nonlethal control.
- Sniper rifle: long-range infiltration and overwatch.
- Baton and combat knife: close-range stealth tools.
- Lockpick and multitool: the basic access-denial bypass kit.
- Bioelectric cell and medkits: the resource layer behind surviving the mission.
- Assault rifles and heavier weapons: the loud route when stealth collapses.

## Example Fixtures
- Liberty Island: Paul Denton, the GEP gun, the mini crossbow, and a handful of access tools.
- Hong Kong: Tracer Tong, triad alliances, and social gatekeeping.
- UNATCO and Majestic 12 spaces: terminals, keypads, and surveillance as a recurring obstacle.

## Narrative Shape
- Conspiracy storytelling with layered factions and hidden motives.
- Player choice that affects how subplots unfold and what support becomes available.
- Optional investigations that reward curiosity rather than linear progression.

## Simulae Notes
- Best reference for hybrid stealth, roleplay, and systems-driven objective solving.
- Good benchmark for making builds matter without locking the player into a single class fantasy.
- Strong model for hidden-faction storytelling, hub-based mission chains, and access gating through both skill and gear.
