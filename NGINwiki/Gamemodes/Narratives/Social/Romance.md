# Romance

Romance narratives are relationship arcs driven by attraction, compatibility, secrets, and social consequences. They should support light flirtation, slow-burn courtship, intense drama, and the practical realities of romance inside a living simulation.

## Romance Beats
- Meeting and first impressions.
- Flirting, gifts, compliments, and repeated contact.
- Confession, commitment, and exclusivity.
- Conflict, jealousy, distance, betrayal, or misunderstanding.
- Reconciliation, breakup, or a permanent change in the relationship.

## Relationship Variants
- Simple crush: one-sided attraction with uncertainty.
- Mutual courtship: both characters are interested and testing compatibility.
- Secret affair: the relationship must be hidden from rivals, family, or faction leadership.
- Political marriage: romance as alliance, peace treaty, or succession strategy.
- Forbidden romance: social rank, duty, religion, or faction loyalty makes the relationship risky.
- Love triangle: two rivals compete for one person's affection or commitment.

## Romance Plot Variants
- First meeting spark: the relationship starts with immediate interest or chemistry.
- Slow burn: attraction develops over repeated scenes and shared risks.
- Rival suitors: multiple characters compete for the same affection or promise.
- Arranged partnership: the relationship begins as duty and may become genuine.
- Secret courtship: the couple must hide the relationship from family or faction.
- Elopement: the pair escapes social pressure to commit on their own terms.
- Breakup and reconciliation: the relationship fractures, then attempts repair.
- Jealousy spiral: third parties, rumors, or insecurities destabilize the bond.
- Long-distance romance: separation, letters, visits, and reunion become the arc.
- Marriage and household: romance becomes domestic, political, or dynastic.

## Player Interactions
- Pursue, reject, confess, or delay a relationship.
- Give gifts, favors, protection, or public support.
- Hide the relationship, expose it, or weaponize it.
- Use the relationship to create alliances, soften enemies, or destabilize rivals.
- Break up, reconcile, marry, elope, or maintain a long-term affair.

## What The Generator Should Track
- Attraction, compatibility, trust, loyalty, and jealousy.
- Public and private status of the relationship.
- Conflicts caused by family, faction, class, religion, or duty.
- Who knows about the relationship and how they feel about it.
- Whether the relationship changes faction reputation, inheritance, or political standing.

## Good Romance Stories
- A noble and a bodyguard try to keep a relationship secret from the court.
- Two party members want the same NPC and the rivalry affects mission cohesion.
- A political marriage is used to stop a war, but the couple may become real allies or enemies.
- A character falls for an undercover agent and must choose between love and loyalty.

## Narrative Payoffs
- Weddings, engagements, elopements, breakups, reconciliations, and betrayals.
- Family feuds, faction tensions, or social scandal.
- New allies, protected assets, or a softened enemy through affection.
