# Become Faction Leader

Becoming faction leader is the culmination of a long faction arc. The player should be able to earn leadership through merit, politics, survival, inheritance, election, manipulation, or a clean or dirty takeover.

## Ways To Become Leader
- Merit promotion: the player outperforms everyone else and gets elevated by the faction.
- Succession: the current leader dies, resigns, disappears, or steps down.
- Election: the faction votes or councils on the next leader.
- Challenge: the player wins a duel, trial, debate, or public test.
- Coup or takeover: the player removes the current leadership and seizes control.
- Regent or acting leader: the player temporarily takes command and keeps it.

## What The Generator Should Track
- Legitimacy: does the faction accept the player as leader, or merely obey them for now?
- Support blocs: who backs the player, who opposes them, and who is undecided.
- Succession rules: heredity, appointment, merit, election, duels, or council approval.
- Leadership duties: payroll, mission assignment, diplomacy, security, doctrine, and punishment.
- Rival claimants: challengers, co-leaders, hidden heirs, and puppet masters.

## Leadership Plot Variants
- Merit ascent: the player earns command through repeated competence.
- Elective victory: the player wins a council, election, or vote of confidence.
- Succession inheritance: leadership passes by family line, will, or appointment.
- Crisis regency: the player holds temporary power during a crisis or absence.
- Usurpation: the player removes the current leader by force, fraud, or leverage.
- Figurehead rule: the player is the public face while another power pulls strings.
- Reform takeover: the player wins leadership by promising to fix corruption or decline.
- War leadership: the player becomes leader because a campaign demands a proven commander.
- Schism settlement: the player is chosen to prevent a faction split or reunify the group.
- Founder's return: a legendary founder or exiled heir comes back to claim control.

## Leadership Gameplay
- Reassign ranks and responsibilities.
- Choose policy, doctrine, targets, and allies.
- Resolve internal disputes or let them fester into schism.
- Promote loyalists or remove rivals.
- Turn faction resources into new missions, territory, or leverage.

## Failure Modes
- The player becomes a figurehead while another power really controls the faction.
- The player wins leadership but loses legitimacy.
- The faction splits into loyal and hostile subfactions.
- A rival claimant stages a coup, election upset, or assassination.

## Example Leadership Stories
- A mercenary captain retires and the company elects the most successful field commander.
- A guildmaster is unmasked as corrupt and the player takes over reform efforts.
- A crime boss is killed, forcing the player to unify the surviving crews.
- A knightly order chooses a new grandmaster after a relic quest proves worth.
- A spy agency promotes a handler into director after a crisis and a leak.
