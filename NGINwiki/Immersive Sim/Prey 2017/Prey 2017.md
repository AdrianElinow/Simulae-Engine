# Prey (2017)

Prey (2017) is a continuous-space immersive sim about scavenging, improvisation, alien powers, and the slow discovery of a hostile station.
It is a useful benchmark for interconnected world design that keeps paying off as the player gains new tools.

## Core Loop
1. Explore a connected station and uncover the next objective through environmental clues.
2. Scavenge, recycle, and craft to stay supplied.
3. Use tools, hacks, and powers to create new routes through the station.
4. Return to earlier areas once new abilities make them meaningfully different.

## Required Systems
- A continuous world with backtracking and shortcut unlocks.
- Environmental traversal tools that can create new paths rather than just combat effects.
- Crafting, recycling, and resource conversion loops.
- Alien or mutation-style powers that interact with the environment and the player body.
- Stealth, combat, and exploration systems that all pull on the same resource pool.

## Factions
- TranStar: the corporate research layer.
- Talos I crew: the human social layer, from scientists to security to maintenance.
- Volunteer program survivors: the experimental human group that creates moral tension.
- The Typhon: the alien species and ecological threat.
- Station defense systems: turrets, operators, and security infrastructure as a semi-factional pressure.

## Signature NPCs
- Morgan Yu: the player character and central investigator.
- Alex Yu: the corporate sibling and narrative anchor on the other side of the station.
- January and December: operator AI personalities that guide or complicate the run.
- Dr. Lorenzo Calvino, Dayo Igwe, Mikhaila Ilyushin, Sarah Elazar, Danielle Sho, and Abigail Foy: the key human support cast.
- Walther Dahl: the cleanup and enemy-response pressure point.
- Aaron Ingram, Luka Golubkin, and Yuri Andronov: volunteer-program fixtures that make the station feel occupied.
- William Yu and TranStar board pressure: background corporate authority.

## Signature Weapons and Tools
- Wrench: the opening melee baseline.
- GLOO Cannon: traversal, crowd control, and environmental problem solving in one tool.
- Silenced pistol and shotgun: the plain firearm layer.
- Boltcaster: low-damage utility weapon for remote interaction and distraction.
- Disruptor stun gun: nonlethal control and robot interruption.
- Q-Beam: endgame beam weapon and resource converter.
- Recycler charges, turrets, medkits, psi hypos, suit chipsets, and neuromods: the broader survival toolkit.
- Psychoscope: the alien-scan and power-unlock gate.

## Typhon Powers and Enemy Types
- Mimic Matter: object transformation and infiltration.
- Kinetic Blast: force-based offense.
- Mindjack: control over weaker enemies.
- Lift Field and telekinetic-style utility: space control and escape routing.
- Mimics, Phantoms, Poltergeists, Telepaths, Technopaths, Weavers, Nightmares, Cystoids, and the Apex Typhon: the major enemy ecology.

## Narrative Shape
- Fragmented storytelling through logs, emails, and station artifacts.
- Optional objectives and hidden discoveries that reward exploration.
- A strong sense that the setting itself is the puzzle, not just the enemy population.

## Simulae Notes
- Best reference for making a map feel continuous, re-enterable, and systemically alive.
- Good benchmark for letting one tool matter for both movement and combat.
- Strong model for survival tools, alien powers, and a world where exploration and danger are inseparable.
