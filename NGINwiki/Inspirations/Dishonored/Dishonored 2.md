# Dishonored

Dishonored is a mission-based stealth-action immersive sim about supernatural traversal, chaos, and consequences.
It is a strong benchmark for powers that can combine in unexpected ways while still fitting inside tightly authored levels.

## Core Loop
1. Receive a discrete mission in a self-contained level.
2. Scout routes, guards, and alternate objectives from the edge of the space inward.
3. Use powers, stealth, or violence to reach the target.
4. Let the level and the ending react to the amount of chaos created.

## Required Systems
- Mission-contained sandboxes with multiple infiltration routes.
- Supernatural abilities that can stack into new movement or combat solutions.
- A chaos or consequence meter that changes the world over time.
- Nonlethal and lethal resolution paths that remain both mechanically valid.
- Upgrade and collectible systems for powers, tools, and passive boosts.

## Factions
- Loyalists: the player-aligned resistance group in the first game.
- The Abbey of the Everyman: the religious and inquisitorial pressure system.
- The Whalers: Daud's assassins and a major hostile covert force.
- The Howlers: one of Karnaca's street-level power blocs.
- The Duke's court: the corrupt political and aristocratic center in Dishonored 2.
- Brigmore witches and Void-linked actors: the occult pressure layer.

## Signature NPCs
- Corvo Attano: the iconic stealth-action protagonist.
- Emily Kaldwin: the playable co-protagonist and ruler figure.
- Jessamine Kaldwin: the murdered empress who anchors the first game's plot.
- Admiral Havelock, Treavor Pendleton, and Teague Martin: the Loyalist leadership arc.
- Piero Joplin and Anton Sokolov: the workshop and invention layer.
- Samuel and Callista Curnow: support characters who connect the player to the city.
- Granny Rags and Slackjaw: street-level occult and criminal fixtures.
- Daud: the assassin-cult leader and mirror to Corvo.
- Delilah Copperspoon, Luca Abele, Kirin Jindosh, Breanna Ashworth, Aramis Stilton, Paolo, Mindy Blanchard, and Meagan Foster: key sequel-era figures.

## Signature Powers
- Blink: the baseline traversal power for vertical stealth routes.
- Dark Vision: line-of-sight and awareness as an active tactical tool.
- Possession: direct infiltration through other bodies.
- Devouring Swarm: area denial and corpse cleanup pressure.
- Bend Time: the time-control benchmark for the series.
- Wind Blast: crowd control and ledge/route manipulation.
- Far Reach: Emily's traversal anchor for vertical maps.
- Domino: chained consequences across multiple targets.
- Mesmerize, Shadow Walk, and Doppelganger: the rest of Emily's toolkit for misdirection and stealth.
- Shadow Kill: a utility upgrade that changes how bodies affect the map.

## Signature Gear
- Sword or rapier: the basic melee answer.
- Pistol and crossbow: the ranged stealth/combat split.
- Sleep darts and bolts: the nonlethal route.
- Spring razors, mines, grenades, and stun weapons: map control and trap play.
- Runes, bone charms, and The Heart: the upgrade and mystery layer.
- The Outsider's Mark: the fiction wrapper for supernatural access.

## Example Fixtures
- Dunwall: Corvo, Jessamine, the Loyalists, and the plague crisis.
- Karnaca: Emily or Corvo, Luca Abele, Jindosh, Ashworth, and Stilton.
- Void-linked encounters: The Outsider, runes, bone charms, and chaos-driven consequences.

## Narrative Shape
- Revenge and coup-driven plotting with strong mission-level framing.
- World-state shifts driven by player violence, restraint, and support choices.
- Characters and endings that react to how much destruction the player leaves behind.

## Simulae Notes
- Best reference for consequence systems that alter both levels and endings.
- Good benchmark for powers that should be expressive without turning every obstacle into a fixed puzzle.
- Strong model for magic-like abilities that remain systemic instead of purely scripted.
