# Assassination Plots

Assassination plots are compact but high-impact narratives built around a target, a motive, a method, and a cleanup plan. They are ideal for stealth play, conspiracy play, faction warfare, and political drama.

## Plot Beats
- Identify the target and why they matter.
- Learn the target's routines, guards, habits, and vulnerabilities.
- Choose a method: blade, poison, accident, ambush, sabotage, spell, or proxy.
- Manage witnesses, evidence, escape routes, and alibis.
- Resolve the aftermath: fear, retaliation, succession, scandal, or war.

## Common Variants
- Contract kill: someone is paid to remove a target.
- Political hit: an assassination designed to shift power or halt a policy.
- Revenge killing: a personal grievance drives the act.
- False-flag assassination: the killer wants the blame to land on someone else.
- Failed attempt: the target survives and the world responds to the attempt.

## Assassination Plot Variants
- Poison plot: the target is weakened gradually through food, drink, or toxins.
- Staged accident: the death is disguised as a fall, explosion, collapse, or mishap.
- Body double decoy: a false target is used to absorb the attack or mislead witnesses.
- Rival assassin race: two killers, factions, or crews are trying to strike first.
- Preventative hit: the target is removed before they can expose, betray, or mobilize.
- Witness removal: the real target is not the only person who must die.
- Decapitation strike: removing one key figure is meant to break a whole organization.
- Ambush kill: the target is trapped at a known route, event, or meeting place.
- Mercy or release kill: an act framed as compassion, necessity, or ending suffering.
- Public assassination: the act is meant to be seen and to send a message.

## Player Interactions
- Take the contract, refuse it, or redirect it.
- Help a target survive.
- Plant evidence to frame another faction or individual.
- Poison the target, disguise the kill, or create an accident.
- Leak the plot before it happens, causing arrests or public panic.

## What The Generator Should Track
- Target protection, route, routine, and schedule.
- Motive, patron, and beneficiary.
- Witnesses, evidence, and cleanup requirements.
- Reputation damage, retaliation, succession changes, and rumor spread.
- Whether the assassination becomes public, secret, or ambiguous.

## Good Assassination Stories
- A noble heir must be removed before an inheritance dispute is settled.
- A corrupt general is targeted before a coup can be completed.
- A witness to a hidden conspiracy is hunted by multiple factions.
- A rival assassin tries to beat the player to the same mark.

## Narrative Payoffs
- The target dies, escapes, is protected, or is replaced.
- The faction becomes stronger, more paranoid, or more divided.
- A new leadership struggle begins because the old one was removed.
