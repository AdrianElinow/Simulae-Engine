# Weird West

Weird West is a top-down immersive sim-adjacent action RPG about a reactive frontier, carry-over consequences, and multiple playable stories.
It is a strong benchmark for persistent world state and chapter-based structure.

## Core Loop
1. Play through a character chapter with its own goals and local problems.
2. Make choices that permanently alter the world, factions, and geography.
3. Revisit earlier characters and spaces with the consequences still in place.
4. Use violence, stealth, or improvisation to solve a frontier problem.

## Required Systems
- Multi-protagonist chapter structure with carry-over state.
- Persistent consequences for kills, fires, theft, faction hostility, and destruction.
- Reactive world objects and physical interactions.
- Faction response and recruitable or reusable characters.
- Top-down systemic combat and exploration that still supports emergent outcomes.

## Factions
- Lawmen and marshals: the frontier's legal response.
- Outlaws and bandit crews: the obvious criminal pressure.
- Settlers and townsfolk: the civilian fabric of each settlement.
- Supernatural occult groups: witches, cults, and cursed communities.
- Native communities and frontier defenders: the grounded social and cultural layer.
- Monsters, ghosts, and environmental anomalies: the weird pressure that turns the frontier into a systemic sandbox.

## Signature Protagonist Fixtures
- The retired bounty hunter: the hard-edged opener that returns to violence.
- The pigman: the stitched-body character whose identity is built around transformation.
- Across Rivers: the Native American hunter and one of the clearest story fixtures.
- The remaining occult and frontier protagonists: useful as placeholders for witch, werewolf, or other chapter-specific story shapes.
- Companion recruitment across chapters: previous protagonists can become part of later runs.

## Signature Weapons and Items
- Revolvers, rifles, shotguns, and lever-action guns: the baseline gunplay layer.
- Knives, hatchets, and melee tools: close-range problem solving.
- Dynamite, traps, and explosive ammunition: area control and environmental chain reactions.
- Ammo boxes and lootable containers: the physical world can be turned into a weapon.
- Occult relics, ritual tools, and chapter-specific gear: the supernatural side of the system.
- Permadeath mode and permanent world changes: not items, but a major procedural pressure layer.

## Narrative and Ability Layer
- Chapter-based story arcs that carry over consequences to later protagonists.
- Occult or supernatural powers as a setting-level expression of the weird frontier.
- Physical, permanent actions that do not reset on respawn.
- A world built to react to player violence, theft, and faction changes.

## Example Fixtures
- One chapter can center on a bounty hunter, another on the pigman, another on Across Rivers.
- Settlement defense, outlaw pursuit, and supernatural escalation are the best testbeds for this mode.

## Narrative Shape
- Serialized frontier stories that join into one larger world picture.
- Chapter transitions that preserve what the player already changed.
- A world that remembers what the player did even when the camera moves elsewhere.

## Simulae Notes
- Best reference for permanent consequences across multiple connected story arcs.
- Good benchmark for letting the same world support several protagonists without resetting everything in between.
- Strong model for campaign-scale persistence, character handoff, and physical world reactions.
