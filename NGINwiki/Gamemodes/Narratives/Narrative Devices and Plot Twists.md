# Narrative Devices and Plot Twists

Narrative devices are reusable transformations applied to an active [[Narratives|narrative]]. They include plot twists, reversals, revelations, dramatic irony, callbacks, false victories, changing alliances, and other devices that alter how players understand or pursue a story.

The system should not generate a twist merely to surprise the player. A strong device preserves established facts, rewards attention, changes available decisions, and creates consequences that the simulation can remember. Devices should be occasional enough that players do not assume every ally is a traitor, every victory is false, or every mystery has a hidden mastermind.

This design builds on the planned narrative substrate: [[Simulae Event]] causality, public and hidden event layers, actor memory, relationships, conditions, faction goals, evidence, reputation, ownership, deadlines, and persistent world state.

## Design Goals

- Produce surprising but retrospectively coherent developments.
- Preserve player agency before, during, and after a reveal.
- Support any genre, tone, technology level, culture, or setting.
- Make repeated plots diverge through different truths, participants, clues, timing, and consequences.
- Let observant players discover, prevent, accelerate, exploit, or misinterpret a device.
- Prefer consequences generated by the simulation over disconnected scripted scenes.
- Allow stories without twists; predictability is necessary for surprise to retain value.

## Genre-Neutral Representation

A narrative device should be expressed in semantic roles rather than setting-specific nouns. A `protected asset` may be a royal heir, a witness, a starship core, a sacred flame, a research sample, or a sports prospect. An `authority` may be a monarch, council, corporation, village elder, military command, divine institution, or machine intelligence.

Each device template binds to abstract narrative functions:

- Subject: the person, party, location, object, faction, event, or condition whose meaning changes.
- Apparent truth: the interpretation currently supported or encouraged.
- Ground truth: the world-state facts that remain true regardless of player knowledge.
- Hidden relation: an undisclosed identity, cause, allegiance, ownership, capability, motive, or constraint.
- Stake: what becomes endangered, valuable, or morally complicated.
- Evidence: observable facts that can support more than one hypothesis before the reveal.
- Trigger: the state change, discovery, deadline, or choice that can activate the device.
- Revelation channel: confession, deduction, witnessed event, recovered record, mechanical change, public announcement, or environmental discovery.
- Recontextualization: which earlier events gain a new interpretation.
- Decision delta: the meaningful choices that exist because the player now knows the truth.
- Aftermath: persistent changes to trust, reputation, access, ownership, leadership, objectives, territory, or future narratives.

Genre packs provide vocabulary, presentation, available methods, social rules, and content restrictions. They do not change the underlying device. This separation lets one template work for fantasy, science fiction, crime, romance, horror, historical drama, comedy, political drama, survival, superhero fiction, or mixed genres.

## The Revelation Contract

Every generated twist should create a revelation contract before the narrative begins or before any dependent evidence is emitted.

```yaml
device: concealed_allegiance
subject: <SimulaeNode reference>
apparent_truth: <player-facing hypothesis>
ground_truth: <canonical world-state proposition>
beneficiary: <node or none>
cost_if_revealed: <state changes>
clue_budget:
  required: 2
  optional: 3
  contradictory: 1
activation:
  earliest_stage: escalation
  conditions: <CND references>
reveal_channels: [deduction, confession, witnessed_action]
decision_delta: <new actions or objectives>
aftermath: <persistent Simulae Events>
```

The ground truth is locked once evidence depending on it enters player-observable history. The generator may change an unrevealed plan only when doing so does not contradict recorded events, memories, physical evidence, or prior deductions. This prevents retroactive cheating in which the culprit or explanation changes solely because the player guessed correctly.

## Device Lifecycle

1. **Seed**: Select a device compatible with the current arc, tone, content limits, and world state. Bind all required roles to valid nodes.
2. **Commit**: Record the ground truth, causal history, eligible reveal channels, and excluded contradictions in hidden narrative state.
3. **Foreshadow**: Emit clues through ordinary play. Clues should have an immediate natural explanation and a stronger retrospective meaning.
4. **Pressure**: Let consequences accumulate while giving players opportunities to investigate, ignore, exploit, or accidentally expose the truth.
5. **Test**: Check evidence sufficiency, player suspicion, pacing, and whether the reveal will create a meaningful decision.
6. **Reveal or avert**: Reveal through the most causally appropriate channel, or allow player action to prevent the planned scene and convert it into another payoff.
7. **Recontextualize**: Update relevant tasks, hypotheses, memories, dialogue, relationships, and visible objectives without rewriting historical facts.
8. **Propagate**: Emit aftermath events and allow them to seed succession, revenge, reconciliation, investigation, faction, or world-event arcs.

## Plot Twist Families

### Identity And Role Reversals

- Concealed identity: a subject is not who others believe them to be.
- Concealed allegiance: an ally, enemy, or neutral party serves another interest.
- Role inheritance: a title, duty, power, curse, or liability passes to an unexpected subject.
- Double or replacement: an apparent subject is a substitute, copy, impersonator, decoy, or successor.
- Misunderstood nature: a person, object, place, or phenomenon belongs to a different category than assumed.

These devices require identity evidence, access histories, relationship inconsistencies, or capability traces. They should never erase a player's established character identity without consent when that identity is part of player authorship.

### Causal Reversals

- False cause: the observed event happened, but its assumed cause is wrong.
- Unintended consequence: a successful action produces a secondary effect through established systems.
- Engineered crisis: the apparent emergency was deliberately created to justify another goal.
- Self-fulfilling response: attempts to prevent an outcome become part of its cause.
- Shared cause: apparently separate problems come from one underlying pressure.

These work especially well with [[Mystery Investigation]], disasters, wars, economic plots, and occult or technological anomalies because [[Simulae Event]] cause chains can preserve an inspectable explanation.

### Motive And Moral Recontextualization

- Hidden motive: an action was performed for a different goal than publicly claimed.
- Necessary compromise: an antagonist prevents a worse outcome by harmful means.
- Corrupted ideal: a sympathetic goal has become destructive in execution.
- Manufactured villain: blame is concentrated on a convenient target by a beneficiary.
- Conflicting duties: loyalty to one relationship entails betrayal of another.

The reveal should complicate a decision, not decree that one side was secretly correct. Players should retain options to condemn the method, accept the rationale, find an alternative, or exploit the conflict.

### Objective And Stake Reversals

- Wrong target: the pursued person, place, object, or condition is not the true objective.
- Rescue inversion: the subject does not want rescue, poses a danger, or was containing another threat.
- Delivery inversion: transporting an asset advances an undisclosed purpose.
- Protection inversion: the protected subject is the source of risk or the apparent threat is its protection.
- Cost revelation: success requires a newly understood sacrifice, obligation, or transfer of risk.

These should be discoverable before completion. If players learn the truth early, the system should support redirecting, destroying, concealing, bargaining over, or returning the objective.

### Relationship And Faction Reversals

- Betrayal: a trusted actor violates an established commitment for a legible motive.
- Defection: an opponent changes sides because world state makes the change credible.
- Secret alliance: public rivals privately cooperate.
- Puppet authority: the visible leader lacks actual control.
- Rival claimant: a new or hidden claim destabilizes ownership or leadership.
- Enemy-of-my-enemy coalition: incompatible groups cooperate under temporary pressure.

Relationship values alone should not randomly cause betrayal. The generator should require motive, opportunity, pressure, and a plausible expected benefit. Betrayal is strongest when the player could have noticed or changed at least one of these factors.

### Epistemic Devices

- Unreliable testimony: a source is mistaken, manipulated, impaired, biased, or deceptive.
- Partial truth: each source possesses a correct but incomplete view.
- Dramatic irony: players know a danger or identity that relevant characters do not.
- False resolution: available evidence supports closure while unresolved contradictions remain.
- Perspective shift: the same events are experienced through another participant's information and goals.
- Recovered context: an old event becomes meaningful after new records, memories, or locations become available.

Information corruption must be modeled and attributable. A false clue should come from a liar, forgery, faulty instrument, altered memory, limited viewpoint, or misunderstood system; the narrative generator should not silently mark accurate evidence false.

### Structural And Temporal Devices

- In medias res: play begins during a consequence and reconstructs its cause.
- Parallel convergence: separate arcs collide through a shared node or event.
- Delayed consequence: an earlier choice returns after enough time or state change.
- Chekhov payoff: an ordinary established detail becomes operationally important later.
- Countdown reinterpretation: the deadline is real, but what occurs at expiry differs from assumptions.
- Recurrence with variation: a familiar situation repeats with changed roles, knowledge, or power.

Temporal rearrangement should change the order of presentation, never the canonical order of [[Simulae Event]] history.

### Payoff Devices Beyond Twists

- Callback: a remembered choice, phrase, object, favor, wound, or promise returns with practical meaning.
- Mirror: an NPC or faction faces a structurally similar choice to one the player made.
- Escalating rule of three: a pattern appears twice, then changes or pays off on the third occurrence.
- Pyrrhic victory: the objective succeeds while an established secondary stake is lost.
- Earned rescue: prior relationships or investments create help at a critical moment.
- Choice convergence: different methods reach the same crisis but produce different resources, allies, knowledge, and aftermath.
- Succession: removing a person or institution creates a power vacuum rather than ending the story.
- Reversal of fortune: status, control, safety, or leverage changes hands through prior causes.

These devices can provide texture between major twists and are generally safe to use more often.

## Foreshadowing And Evidence

Each major revelation should have a clue budget appropriate to its importance and campaign length.

- Foundation clues establish facts that are useful even without the twist.
- Behavioral clues expose unusual choices, absences, loyalties, or knowledge.
- Material clues arise from objects, locations, records, injuries, resources, or event traces.
- Relational clues arise from who trusts, fears, protects, avoids, pays, or remembers whom.
- Systemic clues arise when prices, patrols, weather, access, law, magic, technology, or faction behavior changes for an underlying reason.
- Contradictions invalidate part of the apparent explanation without directly stating the truth.
- Confirmation clues become legible only after a player forms and tests the correct hypothesis.

For a major twist, at least two independent clue paths should be available. Critical clues should not depend on one NPC surviving, one dialogue choice, or one successful skill check. Missed clues can migrate into records, rumors, replacement witnesses, environmental traces, or faction reactions, but their form and reliability may change.

## Surprise, Fairness, And Agency Tests

Before activation, score the candidate device against these tests:

- Causal validity: can every required fact be traced through existing or committed events?
- Retrospective legibility: can players identify earlier clues after the reveal?
- Prospective discoverability: could attentive players have formed the correct hypothesis beforehand?
- Non-contradiction: does the truth preserve all authoritative observations and established rules?
- Character validity: do motives follow from memory, relationships, goals, pressure, and capability?
- Decision value: does the reveal create at least two materially distinct responses?
- Interception: can players expose, avert, accelerate, redirect, or exploit the development?
- Consequence value: will later world state meaningfully reflect the outcome?
- Tone fit: is the device compatible with the selected genre, intensity, and content boundaries?
- Novelty: is it sufficiently different from devices recently used for this player or campaign?

A candidate that fails causal validity, non-contradiction, decision value, or tone fit should be rejected. Low discoverability can be acceptable for a minor surprise, but not for a central mystery solution.

## Pacing And Occasional Use

The narrative director should maintain device budgets rather than rolling independently for every arc.

- Reserve major twists for transitions, climaxes, or moments when they materially alter play.
- Use minor revelations and callbacks between major devices.
- Establish stretches of reliable cause and effect so trust can form.
- Apply cooldowns by device family, subject, and narrative scale.
- Reduce twist likelihood after a dense sequence of betrayals, false resolutions, or identity reveals.
- Increase eligibility when unresolved contradictions, secrets, debts, rival claims, or hidden beneficiaries naturally accumulate.
- Never require a twist to complete a narrative; a straightforward payoff should remain valid.

Suggested default cadence is no more than one major device per substantial arc, with some arcs receiving none. Campaign-level twists should be rarer and require broader foreshadowing. Minor callbacks, complications, and reversals can occur more frequently when they arise from player action.

## Replayability Model

Replayability should come from recombination and simulation, not merely selecting a different culprit from a list. A device instance can vary along independent axes:

- Device family and specific template.
- Bound subjects, beneficiaries, witnesses, and affected factions.
- Ground truth and apparent truth pairing.
- Motive, pressure, method, and opportunity.
- Clue channels, reliability, ordering, and accessibility.
- Reveal timing and channel.
- Player foreknowledge and which actors share it.
- Whether the device is discovered, prevented, triggered early, concealed, or misattributed.
- Decision options created by current abilities, relationships, resources, and policies.
- Local, factional, regional, or campaign-scale aftermath.

The director should track a per-player and per-campaign device history. It should avoid repeating the same semantic pattern even when the surface fiction differs. A secret prince and a lost corporate heir are the same hidden-lineage pattern for novelty purposes.

Repeated scenarios should also permit truth variants. A missing expedition may be trapped, defected, transformed, conducting a cover-up, protecting a discovery, or never have reached the reported destination. Every variant must be committed before dependent clues appear and must obey the same underlying simulation rules.

## Compatibility With Planned Gamemodes

- [[Mystery Investigation]] uses fixed ground truth, contradiction-generated tasks, evidence provenance, false resolutions, and proof thresholds.
- [[Political Intrigue]] and [[Intrigue]] use secret alliances, puppet authorities, defections, scandals, kingmakers, and manufactured villains through public/private knowledge layers.
- [[Heist Operations]] uses wrong-target, inside-agent, body-double, delayed-security, and delivery-inversion devices across a branching [[Plan]].
- [[Insurgency and Rebellion]] uses engineered crises, double agents, compromised leaders, false flags, and shifting legitimacy, while preserving uncertainty about who knows what.
- [[Faction Questlines]] uses hidden agendas, rival claimants, corrupted ideals, defections, leadership tests, and callbacks to earlier service.
- [[BecomeFactionLeader]] uses succession, figurehead rule, founder return, secret constituency, and legitimacy reversals that continue after accession.
- [[Romance]] uses concealed commitments, conflicting duties, mistaken assumptions, rival motives, public/private relationship status, and earned reconciliation without assuming betrayal.
- [[Assassination Plots]] uses decoys, false flags, staged deaths, unexpected beneficiaries, rival operators, and succession aftermath.
- [[World Events]] can be an apparent natural crisis with a systemic, political, supernatural, technological, or accidental cause selected by the setting pack.
- [[Player Initiated Events]] provide the strongest callbacks: a small theft, rescue, promise, repair, insult, or act of mercy can later supply the cause, clue, ally, vulnerability, or solution.
- Hidden-role gamemodes use role uncertainty and information asymmetry at match scale. Their principles can inform narrative secrecy, but campaign truths must remain causally committed and should not copy match mechanics indiscriminately.

## Reference Experiences And Replicable Goals

The following well-known examples are useful as capability targets. The system should reproduce their structural strengths without copying characters, settings, dialogue, or exact plots. This section necessarily contains broad spoilers.

- *Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic*: an identity reveal recontextualizes player history and makes the player's response more important than surprise alone. Target capability: player-linked hidden identity with extensive foreshadowing and post-reveal agency.
- *BioShock*: an apparently ordinary interaction convention is revealed as a control mechanism. Target capability: a repeated motif that is mechanically meaningful, retrospectively obvious, and tied to player action.
- *The Sixth Sense*: repeated scenes remain factually consistent while their assumed social context changes. Target capability: strict viewpoint control and clues with innocent first interpretations.
- *The Prestige*: rival explanations, unreliable accounts, sacrifice, and repeated visual motifs converge into a causal explanation. Target capability: layered testimony and multiple related reveals that obey one committed history.
- *Arrival*: presentation order changes the meaning of events without changing what happened. Target capability: perspective or memory rules that justify temporal recontextualization.
- *Disco Elysium*: identity and history emerge through investigation, unreliable self-knowledge, skills, and player-selected interpretation. Target capability: fragmented memory recovery that changes role-play options without invalidating player authorship.
- *Outer Wilds*: knowledge itself is progression, and discoveries recontextualize familiar locations and events. Target capability: a solvable causal world where information unlocks action without requiring conventional item gates.
- *Return of the Obra Dinn*: the player reconstructs a fixed truth from partial, distributed evidence. Target capability: evidence provenance, hypothesis testing, and confirmation thresholds without adaptive culprit swapping.
- *NieR: Automata*: later perspectives expose missing motives and information behind previously experienced events. Target capability: perspective shifts that add context and new decisions while preserving prior outcomes.
- *The Good Place*: a premise reversal opens a larger character and systems arc rather than serving as an ending. Target capability: a major reveal that changes the core loop and supports sustained aftermath.
- *Parasite*: control, status, and apparent advantage reverse repeatedly through space, secrecy, and competing needs. Target capability: escalating reversals grounded in location access, hidden relationships, and material pressure.
- *Game of Thrones* and *A Song of Ice and Fire*: deaths and betrayals matter because succession, allegiance, legitimacy, and retaliation continue systemically. Target capability: removing a major actor produces a power vacuum and divergent faction responses rather than a narrative reset.
- *Blood on the Clocktower*: information can be true, false, or compromised for explicit mechanical reasons, and players reason about reliability as well as content. Target capability: attributable information corruption and asymmetric knowledge with internally valid rules.
- *Hades*: repeated attempts reveal new relationship scenes, callbacks, variants, and progression while preserving memory of prior runs. Target capability: repetition-aware dialogue and stateful narrative variation rather than amnesia between cycles.

## Failure Modes To Avoid

- Random betrayal with no motive, pressure, opportunity, or prior signal.
- Culprit swapping after the player solves a mystery.
- A reveal delivered only through exposition when it could be discovered through play.
- False clues with no in-world source or explanation.
- Constant escalation until every institution is secretly controlled by a larger conspiracy.
- Negating player victories instead of adding a consequence or new choice.
- Reviving, replacing, or duplicating characters without respecting established evidence and setting rules.
- Making every helpful NPC compromised, which teaches players not to form attachments.
- Hiding required information behind a single fragile interaction.
- Treating genre vocabulary as narrative structure and creating separate hard-coded systems per setting.
- Reveals that change facts but do not change objectives, relationships, or possible actions.
- Personalized identity, romance, trauma, or loss revelations that override player-defined boundaries.

## Concrete Implementation Architecture

The narrative-device system should be implemented as a deterministic orchestration service around existing [[SimulaeNode]], [[Simulae Event]], [[Fact]], [[Simulae Condition]], [[Task]], [[Plan]], relation, and memory systems. Generated prose can describe an approved narrative beat, but it must not invent canonical facts, select the hidden truth, or mutate world state directly.

### Runtime Components

- **Narrative registry** loads versioned arc templates, device templates, genre packs, and validation rules.
- **World-state query service** resolves [[Selector]] and [[Simulae Condition]] expressions against nodes, events, relations, memories, tasks, and current pressures.
- **Narrative director** decides whether a narrative needs a device and ranks eligible templates using pacing, novelty, tone, and state fit.
- **Role binder** assigns real [[SimulaeNode]] IDs to abstract roles such as `subject`, `apparent_culprit`, `beneficiary`, `witness`, and `protected_asset`.
- **Truth ledger** stores immutable canonical propositions and the event IDs that establish them.
- **Knowledge ledger** stores which actor or player knows, believes, suspects, or has disproved each proposition and why.
- **Clue planner** chooses clue slots, independent delivery paths, redundancy, and reveal thresholds.
- **Beat scheduler** listens for events and advances device state when activation conditions become true.
- **Task adapter** creates, modifies, succeeds, fails, or retires [[Task]] and [[Plan]] structures when the device changes objectives.
- **Presentation adapter** converts an approved beat into dialogue, documents, notifications, environmental details, animation cues, or UI updates using the active genre pack.
- **Consequence propagator** emits authoritative [[Simulae Event]] records and updates memories, relations, reputation, ownership, access, faction state, and future narrative eligibility.
- **Narrative debugger** explains why a template was selected, which clues exist, what each participant knows, and why a reveal is or is not currently legal.

These can begin as modules in one narrative service. They should be separate responsibilities even before they become separate runtime services.

### Device Template Format

Device templates should be authored as data and evaluated with the same condition machinery used by planning.

```yaml
id: concealed_allegiance.v1
family: relationship_reversal
semantic_tags: [concealed_allegiance, trust_reversal]
scale: [short_arc, long_arc]
required_roles:
  subject:
    node_types: [POI]
    conditions:
      - relation.trust_with_player >= 0.35
      - checks.is_available == true
  hidden_beneficiary:
    node_types: [POI, FAC]
    conditions:
      - id != subject.id
constraints:
  - subject has motive benefiting hidden_beneficiary
  - subject has opportunity to conceal actions
  - no authoritative fact already proves public allegiance
truth_factory:
  - create fact(subject, serves, hidden_beneficiary)
clue_slots:
  - type: material
    independence_group: transaction
    required: true
  - type: behavioral
    independence_group: schedule
    required: true
  - type: testimony
    independence_group: social
    required: false
reveal_conditions:
  - discovered_clue_groups >= 2
  - current_stage >= escalation
reveal_channels: [deduction, witnessed_action, confession, recovered_record]
post_reveal_branches: [expose, confront, recruit, conceal, exploit]
cooldowns:
  family_arcs: 3
  subject_arcs: 5
```

Templates should declare required mechanics rather than assume them. A template requiring forged evidence is ineligible if the setting pack has no valid forgery method, equivalent deception mechanic, or actor capable of producing it.

### World-State Snapshot And Candidate Generation

The director should evaluate a stable snapshot rather than querying a changing world throughout selection.

1. Capture relevant nodes, active narratives, recent events, tasks, relations, knowledge claims, world pressures, content limits, and device history.
2. Determine whether the active arc has an open device slot and sufficient remaining duration.
3. Filter templates by node types, scale, genre capabilities, content profile, cooldowns, and hard constraints.
4. Ask the role binder for valid bindings for each remaining template.
5. Validate each binding against canonical facts and event history.
6. Score valid candidates.
7. Select from the highest-scoring band with seeded weighted randomness.
8. Commit the selected truth and bindings atomically before emitting any clues.

Seeded randomness should use campaign ID, narrative ID, device-slot ID, and a stored generation nonce. This makes a narrative reproducible for debugging without making every campaign choose the same result.

### Candidate Scoring

A simple initial score can be implemented before any learned model is considered:

```text
score =
    2.0 * state_fit
  + 1.5 * consequence_potential
  + 1.5 * player_interception
  + 1.0 * clue_channel_availability
  + 1.0 * tone_fit
  + 0.8 * unresolved_thread_reuse
  + 0.5 * preferred_pacing_fit
  - 2.0 * semantic_repetition
  - 1.5 * recent_twist_density
  - 1.0 * fragility_risk
  - 1.0 * required_content_cost
```

Hard failures are filtered before scoring. Scores should be normalized from zero to one per term and logged. The director should select among a small top band rather than always choosing the maximum, which preserves variation without permitting incoherent low-scoring devices.

### Role Binding

Role binding is a constrained graph-matching problem. Template roles define node types and conditions; relationships between roles define graph edges that must exist or can plausibly be created.

For each candidate template:

1. Query the most constrained role first, such as a trusted actor with a debt to a hostile faction.
2. Build candidate lists using [[Selector]] conditions.
3. Remove nodes reserved by incompatible active narratives.
4. Join candidate lists through required relations, event history, location reachability, and capability checks.
5. Reject bindings that require an actor to know facts absent from their memory.
6. Reject bindings whose motive has lower utility than obvious non-secret alternatives unless an explicit pressure explains the choice.
7. Rank surviving bindings by existing narrative relevance so recurring characters are used when appropriate, while retaining some capacity for new participants.

Bound actors receive private goals through [[Plan]] and [[Task]] structures. They should pursue these goals through ordinary capabilities. The narrative device observes their actions and schedules necessary beats; it should not teleport them or force impossible behavior to protect the plot.

### Truth And Knowledge Separation

Canonical truth and perceived truth must be queried through different APIs.

- `GetCanonicalFact(fact_id)` is available only to authoritative simulation and narrative validation.
- `GetKnowledge(knower_id, subject_id)` returns memories and claims available to that actor.
- `CanCommunicate(source_id, fact_id, recipient_id)` checks whether the source knows the fact, can access the recipient, and is willing and able to disclose it.
- `ObserveEvent(observer_id, event_id)` derives a memory from the observer's position, senses, attention, and event visibility.
- `ReviseBelief(knower_id, fact_id, evidence)` updates confidence and provenance without changing canonical truth.

NPC planners and dialogue generation must use the knowledge API, never the truth ledger. This prevents accidental omniscience and makes dramatic irony, lies, mistakes, and partial perspectives possible.

### Clue Planning And Realization

The clue planner first reserves semantic clue slots, then realizes them through available world mechanics.

1. Select at least two independent evidence groups for a major reveal.
2. Bind each group to an existing cause event or schedule an event that can naturally produce it.
3. Choose a carrier: actor memory, object, location state, record, transaction, injury, resource change, schedule deviation, or faction response.
4. Add one or more discovery methods supported by player abilities.
5. Estimate fragility from carrier destruction, actor death, inaccessible locations, skill gates, and deadlines.
6. Create fallback slots for required clues without duplicating the exact clue.
7. Emit a `ClueCreated` event and later a `ClueObserved`, `ClueCommunicated`, `ClueAltered`, or `ClueDestroyed` event as play changes it.

Fallbacks preserve the underlying evidence group. If a witness dies, their testimony might survive as a message or motivate another witness, but a physical clue should not spontaneously become unrelated testimony simply to rescue the plot.

Presentation can then be generated from a constrained payload:

```json
{
  "presentation_request": {
    "channel": "environmental_inspection",
    "canonical_fact_ids": ["fact_220"],
    "permitted_observation": "The seal was applied after the container was damaged.",
    "observer_knowledge_ids": ["claim_8", "claim_12"],
    "tone_tags": ["restrained", "uneasy"],
    "setting_terms": {
      "seal": "approved localized term",
      "container": "approved localized term"
    },
    "forbidden_disclosures": ["contained_threat_identity"]
  }
}
```

The generated result should be checked for unsupported named entities, facts, motives, and disclosures before it reaches the player.

### Event-Driven Device State Machine

Each device should advance through explicit states:

```text
proposed -> committed -> foreshadowing -> eligible
eligible -> revealed -> aftermath -> retired
eligible -> discovered_early -> aftermath -> retired
foreshadowing -> averted -> aftermath -> retired
foreshadowing -> invalidated_safely -> retired
```

The beat scheduler subscribes to relevant event types instead of polling every active device after every simulation tick. For example:

- `TaskCompleted` may expose a clue or satisfy a reveal condition.
- `NodeDestroyed` may activate clue fallback logic.
- `ActorDied` may transfer a role, cancel a confession, or begin succession aftermath.
- `RelationChanged` may make betrayal eligible or invalidate its motive.
- `LocationEntered` may deliver environmental evidence.
- `KnowledgeClaimUpdated` may enable accusation, confrontation, or voluntary disclosure tasks.
- `DeadlineReached` may force an action by a bound actor or convert the device into a missed-opportunity aftermath.

Every transition should append an event with `cause_event_ids`. Device state can be rebuilt from a snapshot plus subsequent events, making saves, replication, and debugging safer.

### Reveal Resolution

A reveal is not a single cutscene. It is a state transition that performs an atomic transaction:

1. Revalidate canonical truth and reveal preconditions.
2. Choose the best currently available reveal channel.
3. Emit the authoritative reveal event and observations for present actors.
4. Create or revise memories and knowledge claims only for actors receiving the information.
5. Re-evaluate active [[Task]] and [[Plan]] structures against the new knowledge and world state.
6. Offer branch tasks such as expose, conceal, confront, defect, protect, destroy, negotiate, or investigate further.
7. Apply immediate relation and reputation reactions for actors who know and care about the reveal.
8. Schedule delayed consequences for factions, locations, markets, authorities, and unresolved participants.

If players discover the truth before the intended reveal, the device enters `discovered_early`. The system suppresses redundant reveal staging, grants the corresponding knowledge, and activates interception branches. The narrative remains valid because its value is in changing decisions, not preserving a surprise at all costs.

### Task And Plan Rewriting

Tasks should declare which assumptions they depend on:

```json
{
  "task_id": "task_301",
  "assumption_fact_ids": ["fact_222"],
  "on_assumption_invalidated": {
    "policy": "replace",
    "replacement_task_factories": [
      "contain_subject",
      "negotiate_with_subject",
      "destroy_contained_threat",
      "conceal_discovery"
    ]
  }
}
```

When a revelation invalidates an assumption, the task adapter should mark the old objective as `superseded`, preserve its history, and issue context-valid replacements. It should not label the player as having failed an objective whose premise was false. Bound NPC plans should likewise be re-evaluated using their personal knowledge; an NPC who did not hear the revelation may continue pursuing the original objective.

### Consequence Propagation

Aftermath rules should be authored as conditional effect bundles that emit normal events:

```yaml
- when: player exposes subject and faction learns truth
  emit:
    - type: reputation_changed
      target: player
      faction: requester
      amount: -10
    - type: relation_changed
      source: subject
      target: player
      trust: 15
    - type: narrative_seeded
      template: institutional_coverup
- when: player conceals truth and subject survives
  emit:
    - type: secret_shared
      knowers: [player, subject]
    - type: obligation_created
      debtor: subject
      creditor: player
```

Consequences should be idempotent: replaying an event during load or network reconciliation must not apply a reputation change twice. Each effect should carry the source device ID and a unique effect ID.

### Genre-Pack Interface

A genre pack should provide capabilities and vocabulary, not plot logic:

```yaml
capabilities:
  identity_verification: [records, testimony, physical_markers]
  covert_communication: [courier, coded_message, hidden_meeting]
  evidence_tampering: [forgery, destruction, witness_pressure]
  containment: [guarded_site, sealed_object, social_quarantine]
lexicon:
  authority: council
  secure_record: registry
  covert_agent: informant
presentation_rules:
  supernatural_explanations: forbidden
  explicit_violence: moderate
```

A science-fiction pack could realize `identity_verification` through biometrics while a historical pack uses seals, genealogy, handwriting, and witnesses. The concealed-identity template only requires one sufficiently reliable implementation of the capability.

### Generative Model Responsibilities

If a language model is used, it should handle bounded realization tasks:

- Render an approved beat in the voice and terminology of the setting.
- Generate dialogue from a speaker's knowledge, goals, personality, and permitted disclosure.
- Paraphrase the same clue for different observation skills or memory quality.
- Suggest optional presentation variants that are validated before use.
- Summarize event history into journals, rumors, or recaps while preserving provenance.

It should not choose ground truth, modify role bindings, decide whether evidence is authoritative, calculate relationship changes, or invent a new causal event after the fact. Structured output should be schema-validated, checked against allowed fact IDs, and discarded safely when validation fails.

### End-To-End Example

Consider the genre-neutral plot `recover a missing team` with the device `rescue inversion`.

1. A world event records that Team A departed for Location X and missed its scheduled return.
2. The narrative director finds an available medium-length arc and an unused objective-inversion slot.
3. The role binder selects Team A as the subject, Condition C as a threat, and Faction B as the requester.
4. The truth ledger commits that Team A discovered C and voluntarily remained at X to contain it. Faction B knows only that the team is missing.
5. The clue planner creates three independent groups: Team A took extra supplies inconsistent with a short trip; its last message requests that nobody follow; damage at X faces inward rather than outward.
6. The task adapter issues investigation, route reconstruction, and recovery tasks.
7. If players find two clue groups, they can hypothesize voluntary absence and gain approach options before reaching X.
8. If players arrive without understanding, Team A reveals the situation through confrontation or demonstration. If the team is dead, records and the containment behavior of C provide another valid channel.
9. The original rescue task becomes superseded. New tasks offer reinforcement, evacuation with transferred containment, destruction of C, bargaining with an interested faction, or concealment.
10. The selected outcome emits event chains affecting relationships, resource availability, access to X, faction trust, and future threat or cover-up narratives.

The same implementation can present C as a plague, legal secret, unstable reactor, sleeping deity, dangerous witness, ecological imbalance, cursed artifact, financial liability, or diplomatic incident. Only the capability mapping and presentation change.

### Save, Migration, And Versioning

- Persist template ID and version on every narrative, device, clue, and aftermath rule instance.
- Save committed facts and role bindings so template updates cannot alter an active truth.
- Migrate active instances through explicit version converters; never silently regenerate them from a new template.
- Store the random seed and selection trace needed to reproduce generation bugs.
- Retain retired device summaries for novelty checks without loading every old clue into active memory.
- If a loaded instance references missing content, preserve canonical facts, retire impossible presentation beats, and generate only safe aftermath or replacement delivery paths.

### Observability And Authoring Tools

An internal narrative inspector should display:

- The committed ground truth and apparent truth.
- Bound roles and why each node qualified.
- All clue instances, independence groups, carriers, discovery methods, and fallback status.
- A per-actor knowledge graph with source provenance and confidence.
- Current state, legal transitions, blocked transitions, and unmet conditions.
- Candidate scores and rejection reasons from the director.
- Tasks dependent on assumptions that a reveal may invalidate.
- Consequences already applied and those still scheduled.
- The semantic signature and cooldown history used for replayability.

Authoring validation should run without launching a full game. It should enumerate possible role bindings from fixture worlds, identify templates with no valid bindings, simulate clue loss, and verify that every major reveal still has a reachable branch and aftermath.

### Automated Testing Strategy

- **Schema tests** validate every template, genre pack, narrative record, and model response.
- **Constraint tests** prove that invalid role bindings and contradictory truths are rejected.
- **Property tests** generate many fixture worlds and assert that committed facts never change after clue emission.
- **Knowledge-isolation tests** ensure actors cannot use facts they have not observed or received.
- **Clue-resilience tests** remove witnesses, destroy carriers, block routes, and verify required independent paths remain possible or the device retires safely.
- **Early-discovery tests** reveal each truth at every lifecycle stage and verify supported task transitions.
- **Idempotency tests** replay aftermath events and verify effects apply once.
- **Determinism tests** reproduce the same selection from the same snapshot, seed, and template versions.
- **Novelty tests** verify that semantic cooldowns recognize equivalent devices across different genre vocabulary.
- **Simulation tests** run thousands of arcs to measure reveal frequency, clue discovery, branch diversity, device cancellation, and downstream state changes.
- **Golden presentation tests** check that generated text contains only permitted facts and does not leak forbidden disclosures.

### Minimal Viable Vertical Slice

The first playable implementation should avoid attempting every device family at once.

1. Implement `NarrativeInstance`, `DeviceInstance`, `ClueInstance`, and `KnowledgeClaim` persistence.
2. Implement one deterministic director pass over a captured world snapshot.
3. Implement concealed allegiance and rescue inversion templates.
4. Support physical, testimony, and event-history clue channels.
5. Add `committed`, `foreshadowing`, `discovered_early`, `revealed`, `aftermath`, and `retired` states.
6. Integrate assumption-aware [[Task]] replacement and two post-reveal choices per template.
7. Add one setting pack that maps semantic capabilities to existing game content.
8. Build the narrative inspector before broadening procedural generation.
9. Validate with deterministic fixture campaigns, then add generated presentation only after the underlying state machine works.

## Implementation Stages

### Stage 1 - Deterministic Devices

- Add revelation contracts to authored narrative templates.
- Support fixed ground truth, apparent truth, clue provenance, activation conditions, and aftermath events.
- Implement callbacks, concealed allegiance, false cause, rival claimant, wrong target, and false resolution as initial device families.
- Add validation for contradictions, missing clues, and absent post-reveal choices.

### Stage 2 - Director Selection

- Score eligible devices against current world state, genre tags, tone, pacing, novelty, and player boundaries.
- Add campaign-level budgets, cooldowns, and semantic repetition tracking.
- Choose evidence and reveal channels from available nodes and systems.

### Stage 3 - Adaptive Delivery

- Estimate what each player and actor knows from observed events, memories, testimony, and evidence.
- Move non-unique clues when content becomes inaccessible while preserving provenance.
- Allow early discovery, prevention, acceleration, concealment, and misattribution to branch the device lifecycle.

### Stage 4 - Systemic Composition

- Combine compatible minor devices under a shared committed cause.
- Promote consequences into larger arcs when faction, location, or world pressure supports escalation.
- Measure retrospective clue recognition, response diversity, repeated semantic patterns, and downstream state change to tune generation.

## Acceptance Criteria

The feature is ready for broad use when the system can:

- Generate the same abstract device convincingly in at least five substantially different genres or settings.
- Explain the causal chain and provenance of every authoritative clue.
- Preserve a committed mystery solution even when players infer it early.
- Let early discovery produce supported gameplay rather than a broken quest.
- Complete narratives both with and without a major twist.
- Avoid recent semantic repetitions across different surface presentations.
- Produce at least two meaningful post-reveal responses for every major device.
- Persist the reveal's effects into memories, relationships, faction state, locations, objects, objectives, and later narrative selection where applicable.
