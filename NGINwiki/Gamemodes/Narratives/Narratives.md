# Narratives

Narratives are the story orchestration layer on top of [[Simulae]]. The narrative generator turns world state, faction goals, memories, relationships, locations, items, and event history into playable arcs that players can join, steer, sabotage, inherit, or resolve.

The explicit plot catalogue lives in [[Plot Catalogue]].

## What Narratives Need To Orchestrate
- Long-form campaign arcs: faction careers, personal identity arcs, city-wide crises, succession stories, and world-spanning conflicts.
- Short-form plots: assassinations, scandals, love triangles, blackmail, heists, feuds, rescues, and betrayals.
- Persistent consequences: reputation, rank, ownership, leadership, injuries, grudges, and memory.
- Player agency: accept, refuse, expose, conceal, redirect, escalate, or abandon a narrative thread.

## Narrative Generator Features
- Seed a narrative from a faction need, a personal desire, a location problem, an object claim, or a triggered event.
- Attach prerequisites, participants, stages, deadlines, rewards, and failure states to every narrative.
- Branch narratives based on player method, witness coverage, memory, and reputation.
- Promote side stories into major arcs when enough state changes accumulate.
- Retire resolved narratives cleanly while keeping their consequences in memory.
- Reopen old conflicts when unresolved grievances, debts, or rival claims reappear.

## Smaller Stories The System Should Support
- Succession crises: who inherits a title, command, or estate.
- Recruit and betray arcs: players join a group, rise inside it, and defect or seize control.
- Political intrigue: votes, favors, forged letters, secret alliances, and public scandals.
- Assassination plots: overt bounties, covert contract kills, poisonings, and frame jobs.
- Romance webs: crushes, jealousy, rival suitors, secret affairs, and marriage bargains.
- Feuds and vendettas: interfamily or intergang retaliation that escalates over time.
- Kidnapping and rescue: ransom, prisoner exchange, extraction, and hostage bargaining.
- Heists and thefts: recover relics, steal plans, or rob a powerful target.
- Investigation and coverup: follow clues, hide evidence, misdirect witnesses, or expose the truth.
- War and occupation: sieges, resistance cells, patrols, supply lines, and liberation.
- Exploration and discovery: ruins, lost heirs, hidden maps, forbidden wings, and secret routes.

## Persistence Rules
- A narrative should change the world, not just spawn dialogue.
- If a character, faction, or location is altered, later narratives should read that state back.
- If the player exposes or conceals an act, that knowledge should affect future offers and reactions.
- If a faction loses leadership, territory, or resources, the next narrative should account for it.
