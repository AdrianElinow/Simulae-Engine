# Intrigue

Intrigue narratives are about leverage, secrecy, hidden motives, and the political consequences of knowing too much. They are the backbone of courts, governments, corporations, spy agencies, and any faction where power is exercised indirectly.

## Intrigue Beats
- Rumor: information spreads without proof and changes how people behave.
- Blackmail: one character holds leverage over another.
- Favor trading: promises, debts, patronage, and quid pro quo deals.
- Secret meetings: hidden negotiations outside public sight.
- Forgery and deception: false letters, forged seals, staged evidence, and cover stories.
- Exposure: a secret becomes public and forces a reaction.

## Player Interactions
- Gather leverage before confronting a target.
- Leak information to change public opinion.
- Protect a secret, or deliberately expose it.
- Forge alliances between rivals or split existing coalitions.
- Create cover identities, dead drops, and misinformation trails.

## Intrigue Systems
- Reputation and credibility should determine whether a rumor lands.
- Memory should determine who remembers what, and who can be fooled.
- Faction standing should determine who can be blackmailed, ignored, or protected.
- Deadlines should matter so that a secret exposed too late still changes the world.

## Intrigue Plot Variants
- Blackmail chain: one secret creates leverage, and leverage creates more secrets.
- Rumor war: competing stories circulate until someone can prove or disprove them.
- Forgery scandal: fake documents, seals, or reports rewrite public understanding.
- Mole hunt: the group searches for a hidden informant or double agent.
- Kingmaker plot: a covert actor decides who actually rises to power.
- Secret treaty: rivals agree privately while pretending to oppose one another.
- False accusation: a convenient scapegoat is framed to protect the real culprit.
- Cover-up spiral: each attempt to hide the truth creates more risk and evidence.
- Sting operation: bait, surveillance, and timing trap a target in the act.
- Misinformation campaign: the player seeds confusion to change behavior before truth emerges.

## Good Intrigue Stories
- A council vote can be swayed by evidence gathered in the wrong hands.
- A governor is hiding a scandal and a handler uses it for leverage.
- Two rival ministers are both trying to frame each other before a public hearing.
- A corporate board is split, and the player becomes the kingmaker.

## Narrative Payoffs
- Resignations, exiles, promotions, purges, pardons, pardoned lies, and public reversals.
- New alliances, exposed traitors, or a new faction leader.
- A quiet change in power that the public never fully understands.
